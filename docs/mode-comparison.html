Short answer: yes, with one exception I want to name rather than round away. HIGH is unchanged on hard cases, better on real prose, better on the bulk corpus, and four times faster. One definite-description case out of 219 went from unattributed to wrong, taking that register's precision from 97.6% to 97.1%. Recall did not move. It is a single line, but it is the one number in this table that got worse.

The two accuracy bars disagree, and that is the finding

On 217 cases written by hand to exercise deep-context machinery, HIGH wins by 19.4 points. On 798 lines of real prose it wins by nothing — it is 0.2 points behind. Those cases were authored alongside the mechanisms they test, so they measure whether the mechanisms fire, not how often the situation arises in a novel.

What HIGH genuinely buys is fewer silences: it leaves 24 lines unattributed where FAST leaves 36. It converts them into wrong answers rather than right ones — 379 wrong against FAST's 365. For a highlight layer that is arguably the worse trade, since a confident wrong speaker is more damaging than a blank.
